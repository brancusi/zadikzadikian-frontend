Biography

Zadik Zadikian (b. 1948, Yerevan) trained in figurative sculpture in Soviet Armenia, where the body was approached as a complete and recognizable form. He left Armenia in 1965, continued his studies in Rome, and by the early 1970s was working inside New York's experimental field of Minimalism and post-Minimalism.

At 112 Greene Street in 1973 he painted a room and its fixtures pale yellow, treating architecture as the medium of a total visual condition. MoMA PS1 records a 1974 Billboard Painting and later appearances in Special Projects and The New Portrait. Richard Serra described Abstract Slavery as a collective effort by Zadikian, Tony Shafrazi, and himself — documented proximity, labor, and technical exchange at a formative moment.

From the mid-1970s, gold extended the transformation of space: gilded interiors, then the 1978 Gold Bricks in Tehran — a portable unit whose stacks he has revisited over five decades. Figuration returned under pressure through portraits, casts, and the gravity-formed faces of Foreigners. Recent years brought a renewed institutional sequence: the Brooklyn Museum, Independent 20th Century, a return exhibition in Yerevan, and the Armenian Pavilion at the 61st Venice Biennale.
