Zadik Zadikian
Work Artist Statements Information

I work between the body and the unit: the face, the brick, the room, the field. I was trained in figuration in Soviet Armenia, where the body was approached as a complete and recognizable form. After leaving, the question of what makes a form complete — and what allows it to remain open — became central to my work.

Repetition is not sameness. I repeat forms to expose the differences that pressure, weight, touch, gravity, and time produce within a system. A brick carries the memory of a wall; a cast face carries the impact that formed it. The individual appears through the rule, and the rule changes through each individual.

Gold entered my work as a surface capable of joining unlike things without erasing their form. Applied across architecture, blocks, bodies, and fragments, it creates a common field while keeping every irregularity visible. Gravity performs a different operation: it limits my control and allows the figure to emerge through material action. In the newest work, color opens the system again, making structure movable, plural, and available to change.

My practice has moved through rooms, serial constructions, figures, objects, and public installations, but its terms remain constant: matter, scale, repetition, balance, and the tension between containment and freedom. I want sculpture to hold opposites without resolving them — the singular and the collective, body and structure, permanence and change. The work begins where form becomes unstable enough to be remade.

— Zadik Zadikian
Short statement — for press use

Zadik Zadikian works between body and system, joining figurative sculpture to room-scale fields, serial structures, gravity-formed faces, and movable blocks. Repetition reveals difference rather than suppressing it; gold creates a common surface without erasing irregularity; gravity and color reopen form to contingency. Across seven decades, his work asks how singular forms can share a structure without becoming the same.

Zadikian's career is not a linear movement from figuration to abstraction. It is a sustained argument between them: figure becomes unit, unit becomes field, and the field repeatedly returns to the body.

Figure and ground

What holds this range together is not a signature image but a recurring structural problem: how can distinct bodies occupy a common order without losing their singularity? The figure supplies the stakes of this question, the unit supplies its method, and the room supplies its scale.

Inside the New York field

At 112 Greene Street in 1973, Zadikian painted a room and its fixtures pale yellow, treating architecture as the medium of a total visual condition. MoMA PS1 records place his work within the period's expanded understanding of sculpture as situation, duration, material, and space.

Gold as a common field

The gilded interventions are acts of equalization, not decoration: pipes, walls, fixtures, seams, and defects enter the same reflective field while remaining physically legible. Gold does not conceal difference; it redistributes light and turns the room into an active perceptual body.

The return of the body

The body was never a rejected beginning. It returns as portrait, fragment, erotic study, archetype, and cast presence. In Foreigners, faces emerge through impact, gravity, and repetition — post-Minimalist contingency joined to the irreducibility of the human face.

From gold to color

Color does not cancel the gold period; it changes the terms of unification. Gold gave unlike forms a common optical register. Color lets a common structure remain multiple, provisional, and open to participation — the block shifts from monument to instrument, from object to invitation.

Art-historical position

Working from inside the experimental field of Minimalism and post-Minimalism, Zadikian extended their spatial and serial vocabularies through gold, gravity, the human figure, and the open structure. Gold, the figure, and cultural memory complicate both categories by refusing the premise of neutrality.
