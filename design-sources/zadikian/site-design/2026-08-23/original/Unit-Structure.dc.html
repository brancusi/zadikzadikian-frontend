Work — Chapter III

The brick allowed the gold field to become portable. The 1978 Gold Bricks project in Tehran introduced a simple building unit whose identity could shift through casting, gilding, stacking, spacing, and repetition. Later stacks retain the memory of construction without arriving at a final building. Their gaps matter as much as their mass.

These stacks do not simply repeat Minimalist modules. They remain materially imperfect and optically unstable; gaps allow light, air, and bodies to pass. Repetition becomes a way to stage nonidentity inside order — the unit is complete enough to stand, yet incomplete enough to enter another configuration.

Path to Nine is an investigation into structure, repetition, and the state of being. Three, six, and ultimately nine point into the mysterious: nine holds the unique property of a digital root — the sum of any multiple returns to itself, a reflection of the artist's long journey back to simple form.

Each stack is composed of 54 ingots; hidden steel rods span nine ingots in three rows. In the final stacks the count breaks the system — a departure in which the structure becomes something more than the sum of its parts.
